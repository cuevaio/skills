# Plan Content

Plan the smallest piece that delivers one useful change for one audience. Read `CONTENT_STYLE.md` when available so the angle belongs to the creator rather than to generic content advice.

## Creator defaults

For this creator, read [the saved creator style](../content-voice/references/creator-style.md) alongside the local profile. A useful idea or personal recommendation can be the entire job; do not force a sales angle, engagement question or formal lesson into it. Keep source-specific experiences separate from the creator's own claims.

## 1. Define The Job

Inspect supplied source material before asking questions. Extract known decisions and propose the missing ones:

- **Audience:** a specific person and starting state
- **Problem:** the tension, obstacle, desire, or question
- **Outcome:** what changes after consuming the piece
- **Point of view:** what the creator believes or recommends and why
- **Business job:** teach, build trust, start conversation, announce, convert, or retain
- **Canonical format:** the source of truth for later derivatives
- **Destination:** platform and consumption context
- **CTA:** the one next action that serves the audience and the business job

Write the promise as: “This helps [audience] go from [starting state] to [outcome] by [point of view or method].”

Point of view, business job, canonical format, destination, and CTA belong to the creator. Infer them only as clearly labelled proposals from existing evidence; treat explicit choices and delegated editorial judgment in the current request as authorization. Ask only when a material decision remains unresolved; a provisional brief can support reversible work. The job is clear when candidate ideas can be rejected for failing to serve it.

## 2. Expand, Then Contract

During expansion, collect arguments, demonstrations, stories, examples, objections, data needs, visual proof, and likely derivatives without arranging them.

During contraction:

1. remove material that does not serve the promise
2. choose one central tension or claim
3. separate creator experience from facts requiring research
4. order prerequisites before dependent ideas
5. reserve secondary ideas for follow-ups or derivatives

For a series, give every piece its own complete promise. A series is not permission to publish fragments that only make sense together.

## 3. Build The Content Arc

Use a **stairstep** for educational material:

1. **Anchor:** what the audience already knows or feels
2. **Step:** the new idea
3. **Proof:** the example, result, story, or demonstration
4. **Objection:** the next reasonable concern
5. **Landing:** the memorable takeaway
6. **Action:** the natural next step

For opinion, story, or announcement content, preserve the same functions without forcing tutorial language: tension → context → point of view → evidence → implication → action.

## 4. Choose The Production Shape

Choose one canonical source and planned derivatives:

| Canonical source | Best when |
| --- | --- |
| Short vertical video | One fast idea, visual proof, or personal observation |
| Horizontal video | Depth, teaching, search, or durable reference |
| Written post | The argument works without performance or demonstration |
| Recording/transcript | Conversation or spontaneous explanation is the strongest raw material |

Do not plan every platform at once by default. Name the canonical source, then list only derivatives with a distinct audience job.

The planning gate passes when the creator has approved one audience, one promise, one point of view, one canonical format, destination, CTA, and visible proof. Every planned derivative must have a stated job rather than existing to fill a channel. Carry forward approval or delegated choices already present in the request. Otherwise label the brief `proposed` and identify unresolved decisions without blocking independent reversible work.
