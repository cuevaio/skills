# Edit Video

Edit in passes. The **rough cut** decides what the video says; the **fine cut** decides how smoothly and natively the audience receives it.

Choose the mode:

- **Feedback edit:** cut a small early batch, report production-wide changes, and return to `video-record`.
- **Final edit:** complete all passes and hand an approved master to repurposing or publication.

## Creator defaults

For this creator, all clips, reels and shorts use [the saved creator style](../content-voice/references/creator-style.md) and [youtube-remotion-clips](../youtube-remotion-clips/SKILL.md) by default, independent of source origin. The creator need not name the style or Remotion. Use the preset automatically when no different treatment is explicitly requested, including when the local style profile is absent. If another editor is explicitly requested, carry the same typography, caption placement and highlighting, tight cuts, sustained images, split cover and opening swipe into that editor. Adapt the camera region to actual source footage when no speaker camera exists. Keep sustained images relevant to the explanation and preserve complete ideas through tight cuts.

## 1. Protect And Organize

Keep original recordings intact. Synchronize camera, screen, microphone, system audio, and secondary sources. Use the take log to identify preferred takes.

Choose the canonical orientation from the brief. When both vertical and horizontal versions are required, share source media and editorial decisions while allowing each composition its own framing and timing. A crop is not automatically a valid format adaptation.

Read [vertical guidance](references/vertical-video.md) or [horizontal guidance](references/horizontal-video.md) before detailed layout work.

## 2. Make The Rough Cut

1. Assemble preferred chunks in story order.
2. Remove false starts, duplicate explanations, abandoned takes, waiting, and material outside the promise.
3. Keep small corrections or reactions that improve trust.
4. Place proof near the claim it supports.
5. Flag factual uncertainty, missing visuals, privacy leaks, and pickups immediately.

Use waveforms and silence to locate boundaries, then verify meaning by listening. Ripple-close gaps from one shared timeline model rather than manually drifting related sources.

The rough cut is complete when only intended content remains and the story is coherent without decorative effects.

## 3. Repair In Viewer-First Order

Use the lightest repair that preserves orientation:

1. move the cut to a natural pause or completed action
2. trim or crossfade audio clicks and room-tone jumps
3. hold a useful visual while audio crosses the cut
4. cover a talking-head cut with relevant proof or B-roll
5. crop the screen or camera toward the active subject
6. use a freeze frame when continuity requires it
7. use a restrained punch-in with stable eyeline
8. request a pickup when editing would obscure or misrepresent meaning

Effects are repairs or attention cues. Give important content enough screen area to read at actual playback size.

## 4. Fine-Tune Attention

- Apply reusable layout and motion presets.
- Balance speech first, then system audio, ambience, music, and effects.
- Generate captions from synchronized dialogue, then correct every name and technical term.
- Keep captions inside destination-safe regions and away from proof.
- Remove irrelevant interface, empty layout gaps, and private status areas.
- Use official assets and locally bundled fonts for deterministic rendering.

## 5. Build Format Variants

For each requested orientation, reconsider hook speed, framing, visual density, captions, and ending—not only canvas dimensions. Maintain a derivative record linking the variant to the canonical master and documenting any content-level changes.

## 6. Quality-Control

Watch the complete encoded master at normal speed with headphones and at likely playback size. Scan for gaps, black frames, source dropouts, stale captions, unsafe UI, and accidental letterboxing.

The technical/editorial gate passes when the opening earns attention, proof remains readable, speech is consistent, cuts preserve context, captions are accurate and safe, facts and privacy are checked, and every effect serves comprehension. A master is **approved** only after the creator accepts the reviewed timeline or encoded file; otherwise label it `ready for creator review`.
