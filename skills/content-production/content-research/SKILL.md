# Research Content

Research to support a point of view, not to replace one. Start from the content brief when available.

## 1. Turn The Brief Into Questions

List the minimum questions whose answers could change the script:

- factual claims the audience must trust
- definitions or mechanisms needed for understanding
- current product, policy, or platform behavior
- representative examples and counterexamples
- likely objections
- useful numbers, quotes, screenshots, or demonstrations

Mark personal experience and opinion separately; sources cannot manufacture the creator's story.

## 2. Gather Primary Evidence

Prefer the source that owns the claim: official documentation, source code, specifications, first-party announcements, original studies, public records, or direct product behavior. Use secondary sources to discover or contextualize primary evidence, not as a substitute when the primary source is available.

Record publication or retrieval date for time-sensitive facts. Capture the exact passage, table, command output, or visual state that supports each claim.

## 3. Build A Claim Ledger

```markdown
| Claim | Status | Evidence | Source/date | Script use | Caveat |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
```

Use `verified`, `qualified`, `creator-confirmation`, or `exclude`. A citation is not enough if it does not support the wording actually planned.

## 4. Package For Production

Save one research pack containing:

- executive summary of what changed in the planned angle
- claim ledger
- definitions and necessary context
- strongest examples and counterexamples
- objections and honest limitations
- quotable passages with attribution
- visual evidence and permissions/provenance
- source list with direct URLs
- facts that must be rechecked before publication

Keep raw reading notes out of the main path unless they help verify a claim.

The research gate passes when every factual claim in the planned piece is verified, qualified, assigned to creator confirmation, or removed, and the scriptwriter can find the supporting evidence without repeating the investigation.
