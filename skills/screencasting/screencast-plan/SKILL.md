# Plan A Screencast

Create a production brief that makes recording straightforward. The central teaching pattern is a **stairstep**: anchor the viewer in something familiar, introduce one new idea, and land it before climbing again.

## Process

### 1. Define The Promise

Inspect the user's source material first. Resolve:

- **Viewer:** a specific audience and its starting level
- **Problem:** the obstacle or question this video addresses
- **Outcome:** what the viewer can understand or do afterward
- **Point of view:** what the presenter recommends, based on experience
- **Format:** standalone video or one lesson in a series

Write the promise as: "This helps [viewer] go from [starting point] to [outcome] by [approach]."

The promise is complete when examples, vocabulary, prerequisites, and depth can all be judged against it.

### 2. Expand, Then Contract

During **expansion**, collect relevant facts, demonstrations, stories, likely questions, common mistakes, and source links without arranging them. Distinguish verified facts from the presenter's opinions.

During **contraction**:

1. Remove anything that does not serve the promise.
2. Cluster the remaining material into teachable ideas.
3. Order prerequisites before dependent ideas.
4. Turn each cluster into one lesson or one beat.

For a series, prefer lessons that each teach one main concept. Three to ten minutes is a useful starting range, not a quota. For a standalone video, let the smallest complete explanation determine the length.

### 3. Build The Stairstep

For every lesson or major beat, specify:

1. **Anchor:** what the viewer already knows or just learned
2. **Step:** the one new concept
3. **Proof:** the example, screen action, or result that makes it concrete
4. **Objection:** what the viewer is likely to wonder next
5. **Landing:** the takeaway before moving on

Use a likely objection as the transition when it naturally creates the next step. An intentional wrong path can teach well when it is the path a learner would reasonably try, its failure is safe and legible, and the correction reveals the new concept. Use that device sparingly.

### 4. Choose A Delivery Mode

Choose the least rigid mode that still protects clarity:

| Mode | Best when | Produce |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bullet outline | The presenter knows the subject and wants a conversational delivery | Short prompts plus explicit transitions |
| Hybrid | Some wording must be exact but examples and stories can stay natural | Scripted precision sections plus bullet prompts |
| Full script | Language is regulated, sponsored, highly technical, translated, or confidence is low | Conversational spoken-language script |

When the presenter knows the material but cannot find the flow, have them dictate a practice take, transcribe it, rearrange it, and derive bullets or a script from the transcript. Preserve their voice; polish structure rather than replacing personality with generic prose.

### 5. Produce The Brief

Return or save the brief in this shape:

```markdown

- Assets and source links:

## Continuity
- Terms, visual conventions, and pronunciation:
- Time-sensitive claims or UI:

```

The planning gate passes when every planned lesson serves the promise, climbs one intelligible step, and has a visible proof. The chosen delivery asset must also exist: bullet mode has prompts and transitions for every beat, hybrid mode has exact text for every precision section, and full-script mode has the complete conversational script.
